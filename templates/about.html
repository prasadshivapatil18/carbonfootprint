About & Methodology

Understanding carbon metrics, emission factors, and scientific assumptions.

What is a Carbon Footprint?

A carbon footprint is the total amount of greenhouse gases (including carbon dioxide, methane, and nitrous oxide) generated by our actions and daily choices.

Because different greenhouse gases warm the atmosphere at different rates, emissions are expressed in a single standardized metric called kg CO₂e (kilograms of Carbon Dioxide Equivalent). This allows us to compare the impact of burning petrol, using grid electricity, and producing food on a common scale.

Why Calculate Your Personal Footprint?

Everyday activities—such as commuting to work, boiling water with LPG, leaving air conditioning running, or consuming meat—directly and indirectly consume fossil fuels and natural resources.

By measuring your footprint, you can identify which lifestyle areas generate the largest proportion of emissions and take practical, targeted steps to reduce them.

How Calculations are Performed

The calculations follow standard greenhouse gas accounting principles:

Core Mathematical Formula:
Emissions (kg CO₂e) = Activity Data × Specific Emission Factor

For instance, travelling 100 km per month on a motorcycle:
100 km × 0.103 kg CO₂e/km = 10.30 kg CO₂e / month

Academic Scope & Limitation

This web application is developed as an educational student project. It provides realistic estimates based on established averages, but should not be construed as an ISO-14064 certified corporate greenhouse gas audit. Emission factors may be updated in data/emission_factors.json to reflect changing grid conditions.
